286 construction fatalities, 2025 Confirmed

MOEL's own 2025 industrial-accident statistics (published March 2026): 286 construction-industry deaths (+10, +3.6% YoY) — the construction subset of 605 total industrial fatalities across all sectors that year. Both numbers are consistent, not competing claims.

June 18, 2026 National Assembly passes the OSH Act amendment Confirmed

The real citable legal event: Korea's National Assembly passed a 산업안전보건법 (OSH Act) amendment creating a brand-new employer duty — foreign workers must complete basic safety/health education (기초안전보건교육) — plus a companion requirement for electronic card terminals at construction sites to track foreign-worker attendance. Fines up to ₩3,000,000; effective 6 months after promulgation.

The deck's "Sept 22, 2026" framing remains Peter's own claim, not independently confirmed — search found a company-level program (Hyundai E&C's own foreign-worker safety initiative) around that date, but no government publication dated exactly then. The June 18 passage above is the stronger, confirmed reference point.

The "new employer duty" legal change Confirmed

Confirmed by the June 18, 2026 amendment above — this is a genuinely new duty, not a restatement of Korea's pre-existing requirement that employers give foreign workers the same training as Korean workers.

Cultural & structural realities Context, not a citation

Multi-layer subcontracting (원청 primary contractor → 하청 subcontractor) diffuses safety responsibility — high-risk work concentrates on subcontracted, disproportionately foreign, workers, while the authority to fix a hazard sits with the primary contractor. Separately, E-9 (EPS) visa holders can change employers only a limited number of times and generally need their current employer's consent to transfer — a real, structural disincentive to report anything that might upset that employer. This is why hiding a worker's language from their supervisor (see the Governance model) is closer to a precondition for a usable tool than a privacy nicety, for this population specifically.

Project assumptions

Target languages

Vietnamese, Uzbek, and Chinese are the original target worker languages. English was added for an English-speaking pitch audience. Korean was added 2026-09-25 as a full interface language (supervisors are typically Korean speakers). Vietnamese is the default on first open.

The core workflow

Language chosen once, in Settings — never a repeated per-report step. Speak or type the hazard, mark it on a photo, pick category/severity/location, send. AI turns it into a Korean report for managers, with optional printable warning labels, and doubles as training material for other workers.

What's real vs. mocked

All five deliverables are Claude Artifacts — HTML/CSS/JS mockups with real click-through interactivity, not a working backend. No AI classification, translation, or report generation actually runs; the "AI-generated" outputs shown are hand-built to look plausible.
