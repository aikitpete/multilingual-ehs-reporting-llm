Who does what, and who's accountable

Six actors in the AnjeonTok information system, each with obligations written as MUST / MUST NOT / SHOULD / SHOULD NOT / CAN / CANNOT statements. Written specifically so a later session can turn this directly into access-control rules and feature requirements — see the capability map at the bottom. Full legal citations in the repo's docs/GOVERNANCE.md.

MUST / MUST NOT — required or forbidden SHOULD / SHOULD NOT — best practice CAN / CANNOT — permitted or not possible

1. Foreign Worker

Reporter / Trainee

The person on-site who spots a hazard and/or receives safety training.

MUST
Be able to file a hazard report in their own language, without needing Korean.→ Report intake product
MUST NOT
Have their chosen language shown anywhere reports are reviewed by others.→ Access control & privacy product, 2026-09-25
SHOULD
Report a hazard as soon as it's spotted, with a photo and one marked danger area.→ Report intake product
SHOULD NOT
Be expected to self-classify severity/category with full accuracy — the worker proposes, a supervisor confirms.→ Triage & assignment product
CAN
Choose to speak or type a report; revisit training content and retake quizzes.→ Report intake, Training content product
CANNOT
See another worker's individual reports, or edit/delete a report once submitted.→ Access control & privacy, Audit trail assumption
MUST
Be protected as a whistleblower — employer barred from retaliation, identity kept confidential — when a hazard report also functions as a report of a legal violation.→ Access control & privacy confirmed — PPIW, 2011

2. Site Supervisor

first-line reviewer

Triages incoming reports for one or more sites. On a small site this may be the same person as the Safety & Health Manager — see Open Questions.

MUST
Log an action (assign, escalate, or resolve) against every High-severity report.→ Triage & assignment assumption — no confirmed SLA
SHOULD
Generate bilingual warning labels for physical hazards found, in Korean plus the reporting worker's language.→ Label generation product
SHOULD NOT
Alter the substance of what a worker reported — only add operational fields (assignee, status, label).→ Audit trail assumption
CAN
View a report's category, severity, location, photo, and description.→ Report intake product
CANNOT
Access reports from a site they aren't assigned to, or see which language a worker used to file a report — that visibility sits one level up.→ Access control & privacy product, 2026-09-25

3. Safety & Health Manager

안전보건관리책임자

The person legally designated to run the site's safety-management system day to day — a distinct legal role, separate from the business owner.

MUST
Build and operate the site's safety-management system — the people, budget, and process needed to prevent harm — and investigate incidents with recurrence-prevention measures.→ Compliance dashboard confirmed — SAPA (중대재해처벌법) Art. 4
MUST
Carry out any improvement/corrective order issued by central or local government.→ Compliance dashboard confirmed — SAPA Art. 4
MUST NOT
Allow retaliation against a worker for filing a report — must reverse it if it happens.→ Access control & privacy confirmed — PPIW
SHOULD
Use aggregated hazard-category data to target recurring problems by site.→ Compliance dashboard product
SHOULD NOT
Use report data to evaluate or penalize the individual worker who filed it.→ Access control & privacy assumption
CAN
See a worker's declared language, but only in the specific context of generating a label or follow-up material — click-to-reveal, not shown by default.→ Label generation product
CANNOT
Alter historical report records — corrections must be additive, not edits to the original.→ Audit trail assumption

4. Business Owner

경영책임자등 / CEO

Ultimate authority and responsibility for the business. This is who SAPA criminally prosecutes, not just the company. On a real site this usually splits into 원청 (primary contractor, holds site-wide authority and budget) and 하청 (subcontractor, often the foreign worker's actual employer) — see Cultural & structural context.

MUST
Secure the safety-management system described in §3 — the named legal duty-holder is this role, not the manager who operates it day to day.→ Compliance dashboard confirmed — SAPA Art. 4/9
MUST
Ensure every newly hired foreign worker completes basic occupational safety/health education (기초안전보건교육) — a new duty created by the OSH Act amendment the National Assembly passed June 18, 2026, with fines up to ₩3,000,000 for violation.→ Training content confirmed — see RESEARCH.md §2b
MUST NOT
Treat the existence of a hazard-reporting tool as a substitute for the underlying obligations — logging a report isn't equivalent to fixing the hazard.→ Compliance dashboard confirmed principle — SAPA
SHOULD
Review the government-facing aggregate compliance dashboard periodically, not only after an incident.→ Compliance dashboard product
CAN
Delegate day-to-day operation to the Safety & Health Manager while retaining ultimate legal liability.confirmed principle — SAPA
CANNOT
Avoid personal criminal liability for a serious accident by pointing to a monitoring tool's existence if the underlying safety obligations weren't actually fulfilled.confirmed — SAPA

5. Government

MOEL / KOSHA / inspectors
MUST
Enforce OSH Act and SAPA obligations, including proactive and complaint-driven inspections.confirmed — KOSHA mandate
MUST
Keep a whistleblower's identity confidential when a report functions as a public-interest disclosure.confirmed — PPIW
CAN
Order corrective/improvement measures and safety-management-system audits; publish multilingual safety material — KOSHA has distributed training content in 10, then 16, languages.confirmed
SHOULD
Use aggregated, anonymized compliance signals to target inspections, rather than relying only on employer self-reported paperwork.→ Compliance dashboard product's core pitch — not a confirmed practice
SHOULD NOT
Access individual, worker-identifying report content without a defined legal basis (e.g. an active investigation).→ Access control & privacy assumption
CANNOT
Compel real-time direct database access to a private company's internal tool without a defined legal or contractual basis.→ Access control & privacy proposed boundary, not confirmed or ruled out

6. AnjeonTok System

the platform / AI itself

The system's own obligations, written so they translate directly into engineering requirements.

MUST
Preserve a worker's original report content unedited — translation and labeling are additive layers, never replacements.→ Report intake, Audit trail
MUST
Enforce the per-actor visibility rules above (e.g., language hidden from Supervisor, revealed only to the Safety & Health Manager on demand).→ Access control & privacy
MUST NOT
Auto-resolve or auto-close a High-severity report without a human decision; fabricate or embellish translated content beyond what the worker actually stated.→ Triage & assignment, Report intake
SHOULD
Flag, not silently drop, any report it cannot classify or translate with confidence.→ Report intake
SHOULD NOT
Be treated as the sole system of record for legal compliance evidence.→ Compliance dashboard
CAN
Generate multilingual training material derived from resolved reports; aggregate anonymized statistics for the government dashboard.→ Training content, Compliance dashboard
CANNOT
Determine legal liability or fault for an incident — that stays a human/legal process outside the system.→ Compliance dashboard

Cultural & structural context

Two structural realities shape every obligation above — relevant to any future use-case work, not just the slide copy.

The 원청→하청 chain diffuses responsibility. Work instructions, safety training, PPE, and hazard-information sharing get blurred across subcontracting layers. High-risk work concentrates on subcontracted (often foreign) workers, while the authority to fix a hazard sits with the primary contractor. Korean subcontracting law is explicitly framed around preventing unfair practices from this "일방적 지배와 복종관계" (one-sided dominance/subordination relationship) — a structural fact, not just a cultural observation.

E-9 visa dependency makes reporting personally risky. A migrant worker can generally change employers only a limited number of times, needing the current employer's consent or hard-to-prove evidence of a violation to transfer; leaving without permission makes them undocumented. This is a genuine, not just perceived, disincentive to report anything that could upset an employer holding this much leverage — the concrete reason hiding a worker's language from their supervisor (§1–§2) is closer to a precondition for a usable tool than a privacy nicety.

System capability map

Every "→" tag above lands in one of these seven buckets — walk this list feature by feature for a future requirements pass.

  1. Report intake — filing, photo-marking, language, description
  2. Triage & assignment — supervisor review, status, escalation
  3. Label generation — bilingual warning labels
  4. Training content — worker-facing learning feed, quizzes
  5. Compliance dashboard — site/company-level aggregate view
  6. Access control & privacy — who can see what, including language
  7. Audit trail — immutability, historical record integrity

Open governance questions

  • Role collapse on small sites — does Supervisor and Safety & Health Manager collapse into one person under 50 employees? The product currently models Supervisor and the Management dashboard separately; worth confirming which maps to which.
  • Report SLA — no confirmed source sets a required response time for a High-severity report; the "must log an action" rule is a reasonable default, not a legal requirement.
  • Data retention/deletion — not yet decided: how long report data (including photos) is kept, who can delete it, under what basis.
  • Government real-time access — this model proposes aggregated/anonymized-only access absent a specific legal basis. A governance proposal, not a confirmed requirement.
